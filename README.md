# Fare Iti

A 3D island game set in French Polynesia. You arrive on a motu off Tahiti and build a life there one level at a time:
- raise your own fare and furnish its interior
- grow bananas, fei, taro, tiare, ʻuru and vanilla
- fish from your own vaʻa
- make monoʻi
- win the trust of a dog, a hen, a cat, a pig and a goat
- open your own pension de famille: welcome guests, sell your harvest and buy what you can't make

*Un jeu 3D en Polynésie française : construisez votre fare, plantez le taro, tressez le bambou, faites votre monoʻi, adoptez vos animaux, puis ouvrez votre pension de famille.*

## Manu

Manu, the little bird from Manu Ora, travels with you:
- flies at your shoulder, and perches on it when you stop
- rides on the bow of your vaʻa
- sleeps when night falls
- comments on the rain and on each new level

Tap the bird button at the top and Manu tells you what to do next for your current goal. If it's far away, Manu flies there and circles until you catch up. You can change Manu's colours in the pause menu.

### Fly with Manu

From level 5, a wing button appears at the top. Tap it and you become Manu, flying over the island and the sea.
- Steer with the joystick (or `W A S D`). Hold **▲ Climb** (`Space`) to rise and **▼ Dive** (`Shift`) to drop. Let go and Manu glides slowly down.
- The **fatigue bar** at the top drains as you fly: slowly when gliding, faster in level flight, fastest when climbing, and faster again in a storm.
- Touch land and Manu rests there, and the bar refills. Touch the sea and Manu gets wet, loses energy and flies back to you.
- When the bar is empty, Manu glides down to rest if you're over land, or turns back to you if you're over the sea.
- **Land** sends Manu straight back to your shoulder.
- Six golden rings mark Manu’s viewpoints: the summit, the waterfall, the reef pass, the wharf, your fare from above, and the whale waters. Each one pays 800 XPF the first time you fly through it.

## How things work

**Your character**
- When you start, choose woman, man or other, then your body, skin, hairstyle and hair colour, eye colour, pāreu (as a skirt or shorts, in six prints), top, a tiare behind the ear, and a beard.
- Change it any time from the pause menu, under "Change your look".

**Stuck? Tap Manu.** Tap the bird (or the bird button at the top). Manu tells you exactly what is missing for your current goal and how to get it, for example "you still need 4 logs, chop down a palm", and flies to the right place.

**Craft menu.** Recipes are sorted into tabs: Materials, Tools, Building, Home, Food, Crafts, Music.
- A red number on a tab shows how many things you can make right now.
- Each tab lists its recipes in level order, with locked ones greyed out.

**Ahimaʻa (earth oven).**
1. Dig it (level 4).
2. Fill it with up to 6 of fei, taro, ʻuru and fish.
3. Cover it with hot stones, leaves and earth. It takes 1 log.
4. Come back when it's ready. With 4 or more foods you also get a māʻa Tahiti feast plate, the most filling meal, which guests love.

**Excursions.** Guests at your pension ask for excursions. Accept one and the guest follows you, riding in your vaʻa at sea. Each excursion pays well, raises the guest's review, and sometimes earns a gift.

| Excursion | What to do |
|---|---|
| See a honu | Get close to a honu, in the lagoon or on the beach. |
| Visit the waterfall | Take the guest to the spring. |
| Watch the sunset | Be on the beach around 18:00. |
| Meet your animals | Stand near two of your tamed animals. |
| See the dolphins | Sail out through the pass. Needs the sail. |
| See the whales | Sail far out to the tohorā (humpback whales). Needs the sail. |
| Learn the stars | At night, tap "Explain the stars". Constellations light up in the sky: Matariʻi, Te Matau a Māui, the Southern Cross and the star compass. |

**Telephone** (on the goélette, 12 000 XPF):
- Order from the goélette any time (300 XPF delivery when it's away).
- Call the tourist office for a guest.
- See the weather forecast.

**Newspaper** *Te Parau ʻĀpī*: from level 12 it comes every morning. It has island news, the forecast, and the market of the day: one product sells for double at your stand and store that day.

**Fare toa (store).**
- Build it near the wharf.
- Stock its shelves with your produce, crafts and shop goods (rice, sugar, coffee bought wholesale from the goélette).
- Shoppers come on the poti during the day.

**Tools and furniture**

| Thing | How to make it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Toʻi (stone adze) | stone, log and rope (level 1) | One more log per palm. Needed to carve the toʻere, the iʻe and the ukulele. |
| Wooden spade (pelle) | from level 2 | Digs taro trenches, gives new plants a head start, and makes compost turn twice as fast. |
| Iʻe (tapa beater) | from level 5 | Needed to beat bark into tapa. |
| Table | from level 3 | A meal eaten at the table fills you 25% more. |
| Two chairs | from level 3 | Sit and rest. |

You'll see the tool in your character's hand while you work.

**Shells and necklaces**

Shells wash up on the beach. Look for the little sparkles.

| Shell | Found | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Pūpū | often | 10 pūpū make a hei pūpū |
| Pōreho (cowrie) | sometimes | a hei pōreho |
| Pārau (mother of pearl) | rarely | a pārau pendant |

Wear a necklace from your bag, sell it at the stand, or give it to a guest. A shell necklace is the traditional farewell gift, so it raises their review.

**Cloth**
- **Tapa:** strip bark from your ʻuru tree, then beat it with the iʻe.
- **Pāreu:** pick ʻaute (hibiscus) flowers from the red-flowered bushes and dye cotton fabric bought from the goélette. You can wear your pāreu.
- **Tīfaifai:** sew it from fabric and thread.
- **Using them:** hang them in your fare (tap Decorate inside), on the guest fare (it raises your stars), or sell them.

**Music**

Make a vivo (nose flute) or an ʻihara (bamboo clapper) from bamboo, carve a toʻere (slit drum), or build an ukulele with strings from the goélette.
- Tap the instrument in your bag to play it.
- Play near your guests and they remember it in their review.
- Manu sings along.

**Compost**
- Meals leave scraps: peels, husks and fish bones.
- Put them in a compost bin, along with old fronds. 3 scraps become 1 compost (engrais).
- Use compost on a plant: a young plant grows much faster, and a grown one is ready at once and gives one more at harvest.

**Weather and your roof**
- The weather can now be clear, cloudy, windy, rainy or stormy.
- Storms sometimes spin up a puahiohio, a little tornado that crosses the island.
- Storms and tornados can tear thatch off your roofs. You'll see holes in the roof, and the guests won't like it.
- Repair a roof with thatch and rope.
- "Lash the roofs down" with ropes and stone weights so storms do less damage.

**The open sea**
- Give your vaʻa a woven sail (level 10).
- Sail out through the pass in the reef, by the wharf, between the red and green posts.
- Out at sea, look for seabirds diving: aʻahi (tuna) and mahi mahi feed beneath them. Dolphins race the boats.

**Beach life**
- Little crabs scuttle on the sand and hide when you come close.
- Honu (sea turtles) swim in the lagoon and sometimes come up on the beach to rest. Honu are protected in French Polynesia: you can watch them, never touch or eat them.

**Night sky**
- Clear nights fill with twinkling stars, the Milky Way, the moon and shooting stars.
- Drag down on the screen to tilt the camera up to the sky.

**Pension de famille** (from level 7)
- **Guest fare:** build it on the roped-off spot by the beach in three stages: paepae, bungalow with porch, roof. It starts with one room; "Add a guest bed" in Craft adds more, up to 3.
- **Guests:** the poti from Tahiti brings them to the new wharf during the day, while you have a free room.
  - A bubble over each guest's head shows what they are hoping for: a banana, poisson cru, a hei tiare, monoʻi…
  - Walk up to a guest and give it to them. Each wish you grant earns a tip.
  - When they leave, they pay for the room and leave a star review. Better stars mean more guests and higher pay.
- **Fruit stand:** put things from your bag on display. Visitors from the poti come and buy them in XPF.
- **The goélette:** the supply schooner calls at the wharf every morning, 06:00–13:00. It carries things you can't make:

  | Kind | Items |
  |---|---|
  | Seeds | pineapple tops, papaya seeds, a lime sapling |
  | Supplies | flour for firi firi |
  | Tools | steel fish hooks, a machete, an outboard motor for the vaʻa |
  | Comforts that raise your stars | a tīfaifai for guests, an ukulele, a hammock, a kayak, paint |

- **New foods:**
  - Firi firi: flour and coconut, fried at your fire.
  - Poisson cru tahitien: fish, coconut and lime.
  - Pineapple and papaya, which you grow from the goélette's seeds.

**Your fare**
- Build it in five stages at the roped-off site:
  1. Paepae (stone platform)
  2. Pou (corner posts)
  3. Frame
  4. Walls
  5. Thatched roof
- Once the walls are up, walk in through the doorway. Inside, the roof lifts away so you can see in.
- Craft a peue mat, a bed with a red tīfaifai quilt, a shelf and an oil lamp, and they appear inside.
- At night (after 19:00), sleep in your bed to skip to morning. Your garden keeps growing while you sleep.

**Water and taro**
1. Find the spring (pape) below the mountain.
2. Carve coconut cups (ipu) and fill them at the spring.
3. Dig a trench with your digging stick and flood it with 2 cups of water.
4. Plant a taro top in it. You can take taro tops from the wild taro by the spring.

A cup of water also makes a young plant grow faster.

**Bamboo (ʻofe)**
- The grove is near the spring.
- Split the canes into strips.
- Weave the strips into a peue mat or a fish trap. Set the trap in the shallows and it fills with fish by itself.

**Monoʻi**
1. Plant your tiare cuttings and pick the flowers.
2. Press coconut oil.
3. Steep 3 flowers in the oil to make monoʻi.

Put monoʻi on (tap it in the bag) and hunger drains half as fast for a few minutes. Vanilla monoʻi lasts longer. Your jars show on the shelf.

**Food**

Tap a food chip, use the bag, or press `Q` to eat. Fei, taro and ʻuru must be cooked at the fire pit first.

| Food | Fills |
|---|---|
| Coconut | 12 |
| Fish | 12 |
| Egg | 14 |
| Goat milk | 15 |
| Banana | 18 |
| Grilled fish | 30 |
| Roasted fei | 35 |
| Cooked taro | 40 |
| Roasted ʻuru | 45 |
| Poisson cru | 45 |
| Poʻe | 50 |
| Firi firi | 25 |
| Poisson cru tahitien | 55 |

**Animals** (none of them are for eating)

| Animal | Feed it | Needs | What it does once tamed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mana the dog (ʻurī) | fish or cooked food, 3 times | | Follows you and digs things up |
| Moa the hen (moa) | coconut, 2 times | a coop | Lays eggs in the coop |
| Mīmī the cat (mīmī) | fish, 2 times | | Keeps rats off, so banana bunches give one more |
| Pua the pig (puaʻa) | banana, taro or ʻuru, 3 times | a pen | Roots up shoots and coconuts |
| Niho the goat (puaʻa niho) | fronds or banana, 3 times | the same pen | Gives milk |

**Plants**
- Coconuts grow into new palms. A chopped palm never comes back.
- Bananas, tiare, ʻuru and vanilla grow in the lowlands.
- Fei only takes on the cool mountain slopes.
- Rain makes everything grow faster.

**Vaʻa taurua and lagoon tours**
- At level 12, craft the vaʻa taurua (6 logs, 10 planks, 8 rope, 6 bamboo strips). It needs a vaʻa with a sail first, and replaces it.
- The taurua is a double canoe: two hulls, a deck, a small shade roof, a sail and four seats. It still fishes and crosses the pass like your old vaʻa.
- Paddle it to the wharf in the daytime and tap **Start a lagoon tour**. Two to four visitors climb aboard.
- Visit the four yellow buoys: Coral garden, Honu bay, Sandbar motu and Stingray shallows. Then come back to the wharf.
- Each passenger pays 1 500 XPF plus a 500 XPF tip. If you go ashore early, they pay 400 XPF for each stop they saw.

**Fields: ʻumara and manioc**
- With a spade or a digging stick, stand on open ground and tap **Till a garden bed**. You can keep 6 beds.
- Plant an ʻumara slip or a manioc stem in a bed. Water and compost help it grow. ʻUmara is ready sooner; manioc takes longer.
- A harvest gives ʻumara (or manioc) plus a new slip (or stem) to plant again. Both cook at the fire or in the ahimaʻa: roasted ʻumara, boiled manioc, poʻe maniota.
- More slips and stems are sold on the goélette.

**Taupoʻo**
- Weave a hat from 4 fronds and 3 bamboo strips (Craft · Crafts). Tap it in your bag to wear it, or sell it at your store.

**Ponton**
- Build it once you have the vaʻa taurua. It floats at the end of the wharf, with a shade roof and a bench.
- After that, lagoon tours always take 4 visitors and pay 2 000 XPF each, plus tips.

**Pearl farm (poe rava)**
- Build it in Craft · Building. It stands on stilts in the lagoon, with two lines of oyster buoys.
- Paddle out and graft oysters with nucleus beads (8 at most). More beads are sold on the goélette.
- A pearl takes about a day and a half to grow. Sleeping makes the time pass faster.
- Clean the oysters when the lines get dirty. Clean lines give better pearls.
- Each pearl is different: grade A to D, a colour (peacock, aubergine, pistachio, silver, black, gold) and a size. Now and then an oyster rejects the nucleus and gives nothing.
- Keep your pearls in écrins (1 plank + 1 tapa, 6 pearls each). Tap the écrin button at the top to see them.
- Choose pearls to make a necklace (5 pearls), a bracelet (3) or a pendant (1), each with 1 rope. Wear them, or sell pearls and jewellery at your store, the pension or the wharf.

**Beehive and honey (meli)**
- Build a beehive with a bee colony. The more flowers nearby, the faster the bees make honey: tiare, hibiscus, vanilla, lime, papaya and palms all count.
- Bees work in the daytime. A hive holds up to 6 jars.

**Island services**
- **Small port**: a stone quay with a crane beside the wharf. Everything on the goélette costs 20% less. When the goélette is in, unload the cargo for a dock fee and goods for your store.
- **Helipad**: build it on flat ground. In the daytime, take a staying guest on a scenic flight around the island for 8 000 XPF. Guests also arrive more often.
- **Dispensary**: Teura, the nurse, cares for your guests’ small injuries (coral cuts, sunburn, urchin spines, jellyfish stings, twisted ankles). Talk to a hurt guest to send them to her. A guest who leaves still hurt rates the pension lower.

**Street lamps (lampadaires)**
- From level 5, build a street lamp in Craft · Building: 1 log, 1 plank, 2 stones and 1 coconut oil. You can build 12.
- An oil lamp burns at night. 1 coconut oil gives 2 nights of light.
- A **solar kit** (goélette, or the level 17 reward) turns a lamp solar: it charges in the sunshine, and gives less light after a cloudy day. No fuel needed.
- An **electric lamp** needs a bulb and the **groupe électrogène**. Buy the generator on the goélette, install it (Craft · Building), and fill it with jerrycans of fuel: one jerrycan lasts one night.
- **Colours**: tap a lamp and choose a shade. Each one is made from something on the island: red (ʻaute), pink (bougainvillea), yellow (nono root dye), green (niau), lagoon blue (pārau shell), moonlight white (tiare). Each also needs 1 bamboo strip.
- Lit lamps and flower beds make your guests a little happier.

**New flowers and trees**
- Bougainvillea, tiare taina (gardenia), pua, nono (noni), tamanu, ʻahi (sandalwood) and mape (Tahitian chestnut).
- You get them as gifts when you reach milestones. Milestones you have already reached give their gift straight away:
  - Plant 10 plants: 2 bougainvillea cuttings.
  - Tame 3 animals: 2 tiare taina cuttings.
  - Welcome 5 guests: 2 pua cuttings.
  - Cook 10 meals: a nono seedling.
  - Make 3 monoʻi: a sandalwood seedling.
  - Reach level 10: a tamanu seedling.
  - Earn 20 000 XPF: a mape seedling.
- Once they grow, take cuttings or seedlings to plant more. The goélette also sells bougainvillea and taina cuttings.
- What they give:
  - **Hei pua**: a flower necklace you can wear (2 bougainvillea, 2 taina, 2 pua).
  - **Monoʻi ʻahi**: sandalwood monoʻi.
  - **Tamanu oil**: pressed from 4 nuts.
  - **Nono juice**: sells to visitors.
  - **Roasted mape**: cook it at the fire.
  - **Flower beds**: for the garden (Craft · Home).
- Their flowers help your bees make honey faster.

**Weather and time**
- One day lasts 12 minutes.
- The weather changes gradually: clear, cloudy, rain, and sometimes a storm.
- Storms keep the vaʻa ashore.

Progress saves automatically in the browser. Saves from earlier versions carry over.

## Look

- The ground has fine detail (sand grains, soil, grass patches) and smooth shading. Ferns grow on the hills, and beach morning glory (pōhuehue) with lilac flowers creeps along the top of the sand.

- Palms have ringed, tapering trunks, feathery fronds with dry ones hanging below, and clusters of coconuts.
- ʻUru trees have a rounded, leafy crown. Rocks are rough basalt with moss on top. Hibiscus bushes are dotted with red flowers.
- The vaʻa has a shaped hull, a painted band, carved posts and a white ama.

- The lagoon is shaded by depth, from clear turquoise shallows to deep blue past the reef. It has moving caustics, shore and reef foam, and sun sparkle.
- Thousands of grass blades and flowers sway with the wind, and the mountain is covered in jungle.
- Tahiti and Moorea stand on the horizon, and the sun is visible in the sky.

## Languages and installing

- **Français / English.** The first time the game opens, a welcome screen asks for the language. You can change it later in the pause menu (☰ → *Langue · Language*). The game reloads in the new language, and your island is kept.
- **Install on a phone.** The welcome screen and the pause menu have an *Install* button.
  - **Android** (Chrome, Opera, Samsung Internet): the browser asks whether to install Fare Iti. It then has its own icon and opens full screen.
  - **iPhone / iPad** (Safari): tap Share ⬆︎, then *Add to Home Screen*.
- Once installed, the game also works **offline**. This needs the HTTPS address of GitHub Pages; it doesn't work when you open the file from your computer.
- **When you upload a new version**, change `VERSION` at the top of `sw.js` (for example `fare-iti-v12`). Installed phones then fetch the new files.

## Run it

No build step: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

- **Locally:** open `index.html` in a browser. three.js is bundled in `lib/`, so the game works offline (only the fonts need internet).
- If your browser blocks local files, run `python3 -m http.server` in this folder and open http://localhost:8000.

## Put it on GitHub Pages

1. Create a new repository on GitHub (for example `fare-iti`).
2. Upload every file and folder from this archive, keeping the structure.
3. Go to **Settings → Pages**. Choose **Deploy from a branch**, branch `main`, folder `/ (root)`, then **Save**.
4. After a minute the game is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/fare-iti/`.
